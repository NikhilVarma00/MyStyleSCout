1. Running shoes vs walking shoes: What's the difference?

Running and walking may look similar, but the footwear requirements aren't identical. Running generally involves repeated higher-impact landings and a different movement pattern.

A shoe designed primarily for walking may feel comfortable during everyday use, but that doesn't automatically make it suitable for regular running sessions.

  • Running shoes: Look for a design intended for running, suitable cushioning, a secure fit and an outsole appropriate for your running surface.
  • Walking shoes: Usually prioritise comfort for walking and everyday movement.
  • Casual sneakers: Primarily intended for regular daily wear and styling, not necessarily sports performance.
  • Training shoes: May be designed for gym workouts and mixed activities, depending on the model.

2. What should you check before buying Skechers for running?

Intended use

Start with the product's official description and specifications. Confirm whether the exact model is intended for road running, gym workouts, walking or casual wear.

Fit and sizing

Your toes should have enough room, while the heel and midfoot should feel secure. Check the brand's size guide and retailer-specific return policy before ordering.

Cushioning and comfort

Cushioning preferences differ from person to person. A soft-feeling shoe isn't automatically the best option for every runner. Try the shoe where possible and consider how it feels during movement.

Grip and running surface

Consider where you plan to run. Road surfaces, tracks and treadmills can have different requirements. Check the outsole design and the product's stated intended use.

Weight and stability

Pay attention to how the shoe feels when walking and running. If you need a particular level of support, consider getting advice from a qualified footwear specialist or running professional.
